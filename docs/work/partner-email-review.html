Partner Email Performance Review

APMEX pays a numismatic pricing publication to email its collector audience. Nobody could say which sends paid for themselves. I built the view that could.

My role
Analyst and report author
Brand
APMEX
Year
2026
Status
Delivered

The problem

Each partner send has a real cost. The data was split between the partner's email platform reports and our own analytics, so the honest answer to "is this working?" was "we think so."

What I did

  • Pulled all seven sends into one deck: delivery, opens, clicks, click-to-open, list health and revenue.
  • Compared creative across sends to find what actually drove opens and clicks.
  • Reconciled email-platform clicks against analytics sessions to test whether tracking could be trusted.
  • Wrote six recommendations for the next send, split between creative and measurement.

The tracking gaps

  1. Reused campaign tags. Several sends shared one tag, so analytics couldn't split revenue by email.
  2. Lost sessions. On four sends, analytics recorded only about half to two-thirds as many sessions as the email platform had clickers. Link redirects were likely stripping UTM parameters.
  3. Missing revenue. One campaign showed heavy engagement and long time on site with no recorded revenue, which pointed to purchases going untracked.

What wins in this inbox

The send with the offer in the subject line earned the best open rate. Vintage and historic pieces with a real price hook and several product entry points drove the most engagement.

Results

  • Fewer than half the sends covered their cost in tracked revenue, a fact nobody could see before.
  • A unique UTM per send and a break-even bar for every future send.
  • Clear creative direction: put the offer in the subject line and lead with a price hook.

Tools: GA4, Mailchimp reports, PowerPoint
